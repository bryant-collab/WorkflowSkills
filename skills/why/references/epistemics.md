# Calibrate historical claims

Code establishes mechanics; it does not establish the author's intention. Tests can establish intended behavior or suggest motivation, but inferred history remains an inference.

| Tier | Evidence | Wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | An attributable PR, issue, comment, or document explicitly states the reason | State the documented reason and cite it. |
| Supported | Multiple independent indirect sources converge | Say the evidence points to the conclusion, cite the sources, and expose the reasoning. |
| Inferred | A reasonable interpretation with limited support | Use “appears,” “likely,” or “suggests,” and show the inference step. |
| Speculative | Thin evidence and plausible alternatives | Mark a possibility explicitly and say what evidence is missing. |
| Unknown | The available searches did not answer the question | Name the question, sources, searches, and access limits. |

Avoid retrofitting a sensible modern rationale onto messy history. Repeated patterns can be accidental. Missing evidence is not proof that a concern did not exist. A documented goal is not proof that a change achieved the goal.

If sources disagree, cite both and explain the disagreement without selecting the tidier story. Treat a hypothesis embedded in the user's question as a candidate to test. Check whether sources are independent before claiming corroboration.

Before answering, check every causal or author-intent claim against its evidence. Keep citations adjacent. Move unsupported claims to inference or uncertainty. Name material gaps when they exist; do not invent a gap just to populate a section. A writing pass may remove redundant hedging, but must preserve qualifiers that describe the evidence.
